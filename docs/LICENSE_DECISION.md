# License decision

Abode to Web uses Apache License 2.0.

## Why Apache-2.0

Apache-2.0 is permissive: individuals and organizations may use, modify, and distribute Abode to Web, including in larger works, while retaining the license and required notices. Its explicit contributor patent license and patent-termination language are useful for infrastructure software, where process supervision, deployment, proxying, and configuration mechanisms can overlap patent claims.

The license also gives the project room to accept broad use without requiring every derivative or hosted deployment to publish its source. That matches a personal-hosting tool intended to be easy to adopt and independently maintain.

MIT was considered because it is shorter and familiar. Apache-2.0 was selected for the clearer patent grant and notice framework. This is a project decision, not legal advice.

## Research and dependencies

Abode to Web's architecture and interface were informed by public documentation for other open-source supervisors/dashboards and commercial deployment products. No researched source code, CSS, screenshots, wording, icons, or other assets were copied. Pattern-level ideas were independently implemented. See [Research](RESEARCH.md).

Dependencies keep their own licenses. Before each public release, generate/review third-party notices and confirm compatibility. In particular, do not copy GPL/AGPL implementation code into Abode to Web merely because its behavior is useful. A new dependency or reused asset needs license review, attribution, and—when applicable—a `NOTICE` update.

## Contributions

Unless stated otherwise, an intentional contribution submitted to Abode to Web is licensed under Apache-2.0 under the license's standard inbound-equals-outbound terms. Contributors must have the right to submit their work and disclose material copied or adapted from elsewhere.
